Pellets in the litter tray: sifting with mesh

  • Waldemar Wosiński
  • Everyday life
  • 18 Oct, 2026

I wanted a low price and a wood-based material rather than mined bentonite, so I chose heating pellets. Sifting them with a small scoop took a lot of time, and leaving mesh in the trays and lifting it periodically also worked poorly. Today I tried a large piece of mesh — it went very quickly, though that is my impression, not a timed comparison.

How I sift the pellets

I use fine mesh bought at Castorama, two ordinary litter trays and a temporary container. The trays normally stand in two different places; I add the extra container for cleaning. I need space for material waiting to be sifted, recovered pellets and the fine particles. The mesh holds much larger batches than a scoop. When I tried sifting the whole tray’s contents at once, material stuck underneath, clogged the openings and required more force.

  1. I collect the contents of the trays in one container; I usually scoop the feces into mixed waste before sifting.
  2. I lay the mesh over a tray, leaving enough hanging over the rim to grip.
  3. I put a batch of pellets on the mesh, pull it taut from two sides and shake it left and right. Whole pellets stay on it; fine sawdust falls into the tray underneath.
  4. I set the pellets aside for reuse and repeat with the next batch. After cleaning, I store the mesh separately.

When choosing pellets, I also want as little smell as possible and no added fragrances.

Sifting does not disinfect the recovered pellets or make them hygienically clean, and small fragments of feces can be difficult to remove. The composition and suitability of a particular heating pellet for a litter tray need to be confirmed with its manufacturer; a heating-fuel certificate or a mild smell alone cannot establish that.

A ready-made alternative: PeeWee EcoMinor

PeeWee EcoMinor is a sensible ready-made option with a factory-built double bottom: the upper tray contains a sieve, while the lower tray collects urine and sawdust. Proshop listed the black/anthracite-grey tray at PLN 164 including VAT (18 October 2026, excluding delivery). The manufacturer recommends original PeeWee pellets, so this is a separate system from my mesh-and-heating-pellet method.

For my plan of one tray per cat plus one extra, buying several of these increases the total purchase cost. I prefer to use ordinary trays. I am also considering clips around the container rim to hold the mesh taut — just an idea for now; the current method is already quick enough for me.

I also have reservations about using a potential food or feed ingredient in a litter tray: World’s Best states that Multiple Cat Unscented uses whole-kernel corn. That does not automatically apply to other corn litters.

What about the surplus mesh?

New, unused surplus mesh could be considered for a moss pole: a tube of moist sphagnum, watered from the top, into which a climbing plant can grow aerial roots. It needs watering and suitable materials; I have not confirmed that my mesh is suitable, and I do not mean mesh used in the litter tray.

What about the garden?

I am interested in permaculture: I prefer organic matter decomposing in place, without digging or maintaining a separate pile. Organic mulch helps retain moisture, enriches the soil and reduces weeding, while no-dig gardening supports soil life. Those are general benefits of these methods, not confirmation that sifted litter material is suitable. I have scattered sifted material containing urine among the grass, hoping it would reach the soil without weighing the plants down. This is my experiment, not proven fertilizer advice.

I have no confirmation that it is safe. Fragments of feces may remain in the sifted material. The toxoplasmosis transmission route described by the CDC concerns an infected cat’s feces and contaminated soil, rather than urine itself. This does not establish that my cat is infected or my lawn contaminated. The EPA advises against pet waste and cat litter in home compost; sifting alone does not resolve that uncertainty.
