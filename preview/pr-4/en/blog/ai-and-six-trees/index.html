AI and six trees: from an idea to an application

  • Waldemar Wosiński
  • Nature
  • 18 Oct, 2026

Beside the former model workshop of the Ursus engineering works, near Cierlicka and Dyrekcyjna streets in Warsaw, I proposed six trees for protection as natural monuments. AI helped me a great deal with writing the emails and turning an idea into concrete action.

What did I manage to do?

  • I collected photographs and my own measurements of six trees. The largest circumference was 360 cm. Don’t ask how I measured it. 🙂
  • On 17 September 2026, I sent the city’s Environmental Protection Department (BOŚ) a consolidated PDF: a map labelled D1–D6, a table and matching photographs.
  • I obtained a reply from the heritage conservator: the site lies within the listed former factory complex, and as of 24 September the municipal conservator (SKZ) had received no application to remove trees from that plot.

A concrete correspondence reference: WOU.1331.284.2026 — the reference of the Mazovian regional heritage conservator’s reply of 21 September identifying the competent authority. I submitted the natural monument documentation to the city’s BOŚ. The correspondence reviewed as of 18 October does not yet confirm designation.

Thank you to Ewelina Wiącek-Piechowicz of the Environmental Protection Department for her patience and specific guidance on the information and attachments required. Her advice helped me assemble a coherent package.

For me, this matters doubly in Ursus: green space is scarce, and buildings are particularly densely packed on my estate. The 2022 ranking already placed Ursus among Warsaw’s least green districts. I want to preserve large trees rather than postpone compensation for decades.

The full timeline and current status
  • 5 September 2026: I wrote to the Mazovian regional heritage conservator, copying the city’s Environmental Protection Department (BOŚ), about the trees’ value and their protection.
  • 10–17 September: BOŚ explained its requirements and requested additional information; I sent the consolidated PDF on 17 September.
  • 21 September: the regional conservator identified the Warsaw municipal heritage conservator (SKZ) as the competent authority. I sent SKZ questions about the site’s protection and any tree removal permissions. The natural monument proposal remained a separate matter with BOŚ.
  • 24 September: SKZ’s Historic Greenery Team confirmed that plot 140, cadastral precinct 2-09-09, lies within the listed former factory complex. As of that reply, it had received no application to remove trees from that plot.

In the correspondence reviewed on 18 October 2026, I found no final outcome on natural monument designation. The conservator’s reply establishes neither that designation nor a guarantee that circumstances will stay the same.

How to propose another tree: steps, measurements and law

In Warsaw, residents submit proposals to BOŚ without a special form. The city asks for the type of object, its precise location, reasons for protection and the applicant’s correspondence details. Elsewhere in Poland, start with your municipality and its current procedure. This guide concerns the Polish system.

A practical package to prepare:

  1. One identifier per tree. Use the same D1, D2 and so on on the map, in the table and in photo names. An address, coordinates or plot reference must lead to a specific place.
  2. Photographs of the whole tree, trunk and distinctive features. Record the date, circumference and measurement height. Arrange access to private land with its owner or manager; explain any lack of access and request an inspection.
  3. Species and reasons for protection, with uncertainties marked. Size, form, landscape significance or documented history are more useful than a story invented by a model. Mark identification from photographs as provisional.
  4. One readable package. A short letter and an attachment whose map, table and photographs can be cross-checked. Check the authority’s current submission channel and keep confirmation of receipt.

Is a large enough trunk sufficient?

Poland’s natural monument criteria provide for a circumference measured at 130 cm or outstanding characteristics of the tree. The threshold for white poplar is 300 cm; for other poplars except aspen, it is 350 cm. Species identification therefore matters, and smaller trees need not be dismissed automatically.

Meeting a threshold does not create protection. Designation requires a municipal council resolution, with the draft agreed with the regional environmental protection director — Article 44 of the Nature Conservation Act. Submitting a proposal does not automatically prohibit removal.

When removal is proposed: assessment and alternatives

A cavity or defect calls for assessment, and a photograph cannot settle safety. Ask the tree’s manager for the basis of a removal recommendation: who assessed it, when, what risk was identified and what alternatives were considered.

Warsaw’s 2025 assessment standard, chapter 6 and page 45, describes specialist assessment and consideration of ways to retain trees. Depending on the problem, an expert may recommend testing, appropriate pruning, bracing or changes to use of the surrounding area. Not every tree needs tomography; a specialist should select the assessment and work required.

Warsaw’s Greenery Authority describes its inspections, care and testing, and the city’s “Wiem o wycince” tree removal map. Start with documents and ask the relevant authority whether an application or permission exists for that particular tree. Absence from the map does not determine whether work is lawful. For an urgent case, give the authority or a local tree protection organisation the precise location and facts.

Where to look for free support and funding
  • A community project: Fundacja EkoRozwoju’s “Dialog dla drzew” runs from April 2026 to September 2027 and includes individual advice, consultations and interventions for residents and groups. Contact details are on the project page. Ask about availability and scope; it does not promise free tomography for everyone.
  • A municipal tree: report the need for assessment to its manager, including through Warsaw 19115. This is a route for reporting a problem, not a voucher for any test on a privately owned tree.
  • An initial contractor consultation: for example, Osprey Pracownia advertises free consultations for landowners around Poznań and Gorzów. Confirm what is included: a conversation or quotation is not a free on-site expert assessment.

I did not find a verified, generally available programme offering free full assessments of private trees in Warsaw. When looking for municipal or regional environmental fund grants, check the current application window, eligible applicants and whether diagnostics qualify, or only tree care. An archived call is not an offer available today.

My proposal: replacement planting by total trunk diameter

I also have a proposal for cities: when someone wants to remove trees and offers replacements, the sum of the new trees’ trunk diameters should at least equal the sum of the removed trees’ trunk diameters, measured at the same height. I would like residents to demand this minimum rather than accept a simple count of saplings.

This is my proposal, not a statement of current law. It is only a starting point: ten trunks measuring 10 cm in diameter do not automatically recreate the canopy or functions of one tree measuring 100 cm. Forest Research shows that benefits depend on tree size, condition and species, and change over time. We should also ask about eventual canopies, growing conditions, survival and the time needed to restore lost benefits.

The idea of “plant small trees and things will be fine in 40 years” does not convince me. Forty years here illustrates postponing compensation, not a calculated growth period for every tree. I want us to protect what we already have and consider today’s residents. New trees are needed, but planting them does not make years of loss disappear.

Planting in planned stages

I would also like park tree groups to consider denser initial planting than the final layout, allowing for seedlings that do not establish, with later thinning planned from the start. My aim is earlier greenery and less risk of starting again with a small sapling after every loss. The eventual layout may be irregular, depending on which trees establish.

Forest Research describes higher planting density as one way of allowing for losses when creating woodland on difficult sites — chapter 5, section 6.3. This needs a local design considering soil, water and ongoing care. Thinning should be planned early, before competition intensifies. I would not apply it automatically to cramped street tree pits; extra saplings cannot replace care or retaining large trees.

A prompt to copy for your own AI

This is a suggestion for a new case, not a transcript of my earlier conversations. Add your own materials, removing unnecessary personal information:

Help me prepare a proposal to protect a tree or group of trees
as a natural monument in [municipality, province, Poland].

I have: [location/plot], [dated photos], [circumferences and measurement
heights], [species and confidence], [documented significance].
Threat of removal: [facts and documents, or “unknown”].

1. Check current official requirements, the competent authority,
   submission channel and law. Give links and the date checked.
2. List missing information. Do not invent species, age, history,
   health, measurements or the outcome of proceedings.
3. Prepare a tree identifier table and attachment checklist.
   Cross-check the map, photos and measurements; distinguish separate
   trees from stems belonging to the same tree.
4. Draft a concise letter. Separate the designation proposal from
   questions about removal and a specialist's safety assessment.
5. Find current NGO support and funding in this region. Distinguish
   free advice, inspections, expert reports and instrumented testing.
   Verify application dates, costs and eligibility.
6. If danger is alleged, prepare questions about the assessment and
   possible alternatives. Do not judge safety from photographs.
7. Finish with next steps and points to confirm with the authority.
   Do not promise designation or a halt to removal.
